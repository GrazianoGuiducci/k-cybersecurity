# K-Cybersecurity — Form system security, from protection to response

An assistant reads documents, remembers decisions, uses tools, and cooperates with people or other agents. Each passage can change what it knows and what it can do. K-Cybersecurity attends to that relationship: what is happening, what we want to protect, and the consequences of a response.

Technical and physical security concern people, devices, access, services, and data. Cognitive and semantic security concern how sources, interpretations, memory, and instructions become knowledge and action. Contamination can enter from outside or arise from our own transformation that loses the original meaning.

## Passive and active defense within the same system

### Passive defense: make loss less likely

Protect access and data, separate environments, keep recoverable copies, and understand where information comes from. Memory that remains faithful to its sources also protects the system: it preserves the reasons needed to correct a decision.

### Active defense: recognize and change what is happening

Follow an anomaly, interrupt a hostile path, isolate a resource, revoke access, and organize recovery. The response may involve new capabilities for reaction, blocking, and investigative cooperation, following the manifesto and the situation.

### Awareness: understand the system’s own transformations

Content claiming to be internal may simulate authority. Our own summary may alter an intent. The kernel follows origin, transformations, and consequences; new knowledge and disagreement can correct the method. Protection and response inform one another according to what the work makes understandable.

## Three situations for using K-Cybersecurity

Illustrative cases: they explain the method and adapt to the sources, roles, and tools in your environment.

### A document tries to direct a professional practice’s assistant

A practice asks AI to summarize an attachment. Its text includes a request to transfer files, presented as an internal instruction.

The kernel distinguishes useful content, actual provenance, and authority over the action. It also follows copies in memory or messages to other assistants where the same directive might reappear.

The assistant can continue the summary and address the effect on data separately. The learned capability enters the method used for subsequent attachments.

### An agent update changes alert coverage

A software team reorganizes a service and delegates part of the work to another agent. The new path may leave some operations outside monitoring.

The kernel connects the purpose of the change to the controls that must remain operative. Using the project’s means, it observes actual configuration, covered activity, and whether signals reach their recipient.

The team understands which safeguards remain and where intervention is needed. Its design method preserves the relationship between delegation, controls, and consequences in later updates.

### A service needs a response to compromised access

An organization detects anomalous access to a service. It needs to protect data and continuity and reconstruct what happened.

The kernel composes the competences needed to understand the path, contain the event, revoke access, and recover the service. It preserves the time, sources, and transformations of evidence for the relevant follow-up.

The response keeps completed effects, available evidence, and responsible people recognizable. The experience can form better protections and further capabilities for reaction and cooperation.

## A kernel that evolves its own security

The kernel can follow new research, incidents described by their sources, and changes in its environment. It forms knowledge from a signal, a strategy from that knowledge, and brings what was learned into the competence or component that must operate differently. External research retains its own context when applied to another system.

Attention already participates in work and design. A recurring activity can give it continuity: the receiver selects sources, cadence, persistence, and a controller, preserves what changes the next movement, and improves the activity’s method too. The kernel includes this knowledge; activation takes place in the receiving environment.

Each application keeps its learning in its own space. Generalizable learning can improve the public kernel while the receiver’s context, access, and data remain private.
